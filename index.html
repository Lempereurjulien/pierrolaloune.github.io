About

I am a PhD student in ecology and biogeography at the CRBE, Université Toulouse III – Paul Sabatier (Toulouse, France), supervised by Sébastien Brosse and Aurèle Toussaint. My research focuses on how human uses of vertebrates (e.g. fisheries, hunting, trade, recreation) shape biodiversity patterns and functional diversity at large spatial scales.

More broadly, I work on trait-based ecology, macroecology, and conservation biology, with a particular interest in how anthropogenic pressures erode the functional space of species and the ecological functions they support.

Research

My thesis examines how humans selectively exploit vertebrate species and how this selection translates into non-random patterns in functional trait space. I investigate:

  • Which functional traits increase or reduce the probability of being used by humans (fisheries, hunting, trade, recreation).
  • How human use interacts with extinction risk and, conversely, with the potential to become invasive.
  • How these patterns vary among regions, depending on local and regional human practices and pressures.

The work adopts a macroecological, cross-taxon perspective, relying on large trait datasets, global distribution information, and conservation assessments.
