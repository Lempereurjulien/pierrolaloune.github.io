About

I am a PhD student and geographer specializing in community ecology and functional diversity. My research focuses on how human activities reshape biodiversity patterns and the functional diversity of vertebrates at global and macroecological scales. More broadly, I work on trait-based ecology, macroecology, conservation biology, and the impacts of anthropogenic pressures on ecosystems.

I am also actively engaged in scientific outreach through associations such as Pint of Science and Les étoiles brillent pour tous, contributing to the dissemination and accessibility of scientific knowledge. Alongside my research, I teach undergraduate courses at the University of Toulouse.

My Work

My research integrates biogeography, trait-based ecology, and macroecology to better understand the impacts of anthropogenic pressures on biodiversity. I focus particularly on functional diversity—the range of biological traits present in an ecosystem—and how human activities reshape the ecological roles of vertebrates worldwide. My projects involve large-scale data analysis to assess extinction risks, conservation priorities, and the importance of morphologically unique species in maintaining ecological resilience.

  • Trait-based approaches to study ecological communities
  • Analysis of biodiversity loss and functional erosion
  • Conservation strategies addressing global change
  • Teaching and outreach in scientific associations
